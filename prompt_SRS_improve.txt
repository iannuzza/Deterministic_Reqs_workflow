Do not leave overview sections empty when safe approved evidence already exists, including evidence previously selected or excluded only because of topic or output limits.
Treat this first as a projection/rendering problem, not automatically as an authority-absence problem.
Populate overview sections with system-level technical prose, not raw source fragments, OCR stitching, table dumps, path dumps, or block-local descriptions.
Expected overview content
General System Description: describe the system or product itself, not the purpose of the document.
Main System Capabilities: summarize top-level capabilities only, without implementation detail, registers, signals, or raw data-name lists.
Main Architectural Domains and Subsystems: aggregate into top-level domains or subsystems, not block inventories.
External Interfaces and System Boundaries: explain system boundaries and interface roles, with tables only as support.
Operating Concept: explain the role of approved operating modes, not just list them, and do not include local control detail.
Power, Clock, and Reset Overview: describe only safe top-level structure supported by clean evidence; keep unsupported portions as narrow targeted gaps.
Scope, Assumptions, and Allocation Boundaries: state engineering scope and allocation boundaries, not generator disclaimers.
Writing rules
Use natural technical prose.
Present meaning first, support tables second.
Keep final narrative free of audit language and evidence counting.
Do not copy normative text into descriptive prose.
Do not infer functionality from the mere presence of blocks, ports, signals, or connections.
Keep descriptive provenance in audit artifacts; keep normative traceability separate through approved mechanisms such as Covers.
Deduplication
Do not repeat the same block or subsystem description across sections.
Keep each section focused on its own role: overview for meaning, detailed sections for structure, interfaces, requirements, or allocation.
Remove repeated block-purpose prose from list sections and data-path sections when it already appears elsewhere.
Formatting
Render structured technical content for readability, not as flat bullet dumps.
For repeated structured items such as domains, modes, interface groups, or subsystem groups:
separate each item clearly
visually distinguish the item name
use indented sub-items for attributes
Improve readability without changing content meaning.
Governance
Keep everything deterministic, centralized, and project-agnostic.
Do not hardcode project names, block names, or review examples into shared rules, generators, selectors, validators, or policy.
Do not introduce new authority paths.
Do not import authority from parallel documents unless explicitly allowed by the approved workflow contract.
